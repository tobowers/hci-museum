**Filed:** `docs/blog/the-memory-you-carried-in-your-pocket.md`
**Subject:** Toshiba LC-836MN Memo Note 30 (1978) — the first portable electronic data bank, 256 bytes of carryable memory squeezed through a calculator keypad, nine years before the organizer category existed.

**Story angle:** The Memo Note 30 is a calculator that secretly stores names and phone numbers. The entire HCI innovation is in the constraint: text squeezed through a calculator's digit keypad onto an 8-segment alphanumeric display built for letters. The artifact proves the demand for electronic memory existed before the hardware was comfortable. The museum's organizers (Sharp Wizard, Psion Organiser II, Casio PB-1000) all sit a decade later as resolutions of the same idea.

**Why this artifact, not the Fehmi biofeedback computer from the same batch:**
- The Fehmi is a genuinely strange artifact (coherence you can hear, phase-synchrony biofeedback) but it's inherently harder to picture without images — it only has patent diagrams and the concept is abstract. The Memo Note 30 is tactile, concrete, and the constraint (multi-tap text entry through a calculator keypad, 8-segment display) is immediately graspable.
- The Memo Note 30 connects to a larger story (the birth of the organizer/PDA/smartphone category) that is easy to orient the reader within.
- The Fehmi is a better candidate for a future note when the collection has more biofeedback/neurofeedback artifacts to group it with.

## What was written

**"The Spinning Disk That Painted 3D"** (`docs/blog/the-spinning-disk-that-painted-3d.md`)

### Subject
The Vectrex 3D Imager (1983) — the first commercial stereoscopic 3D gaming device in history. A DC motor, a spinning half-black/half-RGB plastic filter disk, and a monochrome vector CRT produced full-color 3D four years before Sega claimed "world's first."

### Why this story
- No existing Field Note covers stereoscopic 3D, electromechanical display technology, or this particular era of gaming peripherals.
- The engineering is genuinely distinctive: no LCD shutters, no electronics in the headset beyond a DC motor. Pure electromechanical cleverness.
- The timing is poignant: demoed at CES 1983 as the future, killed by the 1983 crash before it could ship in volume. Only ~2,000-3,000 units.
- Strong visual hook: a spinning disk, a monochrome tube, and the brain assembling color 3D from the combination.
- Sega retraction story adds historical weight.
- Distinct from existing posts: the-machine-that-pushed-back (haptics), shouting-at-the-future (voice control), the-mouse-that-learned-to-sing (musical instrument). No overlap.

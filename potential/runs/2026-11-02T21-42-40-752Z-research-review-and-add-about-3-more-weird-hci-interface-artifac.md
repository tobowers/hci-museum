## Follow-up: GVG bundle built (2026-11-02, later)

- **Grass Valley 110 Production Switcher** — research bundle written to `potential/grass-valley-110/` with `info.json` + 2 downloaded images. Both images confirmed **public domain** on Commons: `Grass_Valley_110_Switcher.jpg` (PD-user) and `GVG200_keyer_controlpanel.jpg` (PD-self).
- Verified primary facts:
  - GVG founded 1959 by Dr. Donald Hare in Grass Valley, CA; first video product (distribution amp) 1964; first production switcher by 1968; merged with Tektronix 1974 (Wikipedia; vintagetek.org/grass-valley-products/).
  - T-bar mechanics: transition lever / T-bar / fader bar transitions between two buses; in a flip-flop mixer the lever position does NOT indicate the active bus because program is always hot (Wikipedia Vision mixer). This is the core embodied/irreversible HCI hook.
  - Star Wars: Death Star superlaser controls were an "ISI/Grass Valley Switcher" (~4.5ft × 2.5ft × 8in), and vintageTEK confirms the GVG "T" handle of a **model 1600** switcher was the lever thrown to fire the weapon (partsofsw.com; vintagetek.org).
  - 1979 GVG Catalog 16 (archive.org) confirms 1600 Series switchers, E-MEM effects memory, cut-bar switches. Catalog does NOT cover the 110 model.
- **Unverified / flagged:** the 110's exact introduction year. No primary source pinned it; kept as "c. 1976 (exact year unverified)" with an explicit note in the overview. Do NOT promote a hard year without a source.
- **Note:** the 1979 catalog describes a "200 Series" as the M200 Modular Automation System (not a vision mixer). The `GVG200 keyer controlpanel.jpg` image is labelled a 200 vision-mixer keyer panel by its uploader — treat the "200 vision mixer" designation as captioner-supplied, not catalog-verified.

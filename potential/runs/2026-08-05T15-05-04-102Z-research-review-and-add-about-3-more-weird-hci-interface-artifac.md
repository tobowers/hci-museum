## Subagent results (all 3 completed, 6 Octen calls used)

### Angle 1 — Interactive multimedia appliances (SUBAGENT A)
- Philips CD-I (CDI 910/205, Dec 1991): STRONG. 68000-based living-room computer; GUI "Shell" navigated ONLY by IR TV-pointing remotes (thumbstick remote 22ER9051, pressure-sensitive "Commander" remote that accelerates cursor with finger force, wired Roller Controller kids trackball by Philips Interactive Media America + Children's Television Workshop, mouse, touchpad, light gun). Green Book: video hotspots, interleaved A/V/data. "Listen. Touch. Explore." US ad campaign; massive flop; ~570,000 installed by Oct 1994; Bill Gates "terrible game machine, terrible PC." 17 verified PD/CC Commons images (Evan-Amos set + Marcin Wichary 180 unit). Empty category in museum (media appliance vs e-reader).
- Commodore CDTV (1991): weaker, stock Amiga on TV. Tandy VIS (1992): Modular Windows on TV, ~11,000 sold, weaker docs.
- DECISION: promote Philips CD-I.

### Angle 2 — Sonic/acoustic digitizers (SUBAGENT B)
- Science Accessories Corp (SAC, Southport CT): GP-Series 6 (c.1977-78), graf/pen (1971-74), GrafBar GP-7/GP-8 (c.1983-84, Apple II/PC). Pen emits electrical spark; 2+ electret mics triangulate by time-of-flight; NO tablet needed (sensors on L-frame over any surface — book, x-ray, CRT); "hypersonic ranging"; patents US 3,626,483 + US 4,891,474. Period materials on archive.org (Ted Nelson junkmail collection): GP-Series 6 Technical Bulletin + graf/pen brochure. Period Microcomputing Feb 1984 review of Grafbar GP-7.
- ZERO CC/PD images on Commons (verified via API searches). Patent drawings usable as primary images (Stompin' precedent).
- DECISION: promote as GrafBar sonic digitizer with patent-drawing primary images; archive.org brochure scans as source leads.

### Angle 3 — Unusual controllers (SUBAGENT C)
- Konix Speedking (1985-86, £12.99, UK): palm-cup handheld joystick, no base, short flexible shaft, side fire button, 8-way microswitches. "Right where you need it — in the palm of your hand." Won Your Computer "Best Joystick 1986"; sold in US as Epyx 500XJ. Centre for Computing History holds unit CH50218. Design Council award + "best-selling" claims UNVERIFIED. ZERO Commons images.
- Intellivision disc controller (1979): 16-direction rotating disc + starburst detent + overlay keypad. Commons files exist: File:Intellivision-Controller.jpg, File:Intellivision controller.jpg (licenses/URLs pending).
- NES Max (1988): optical sensor joystick, Commons images exist but NES peripheral category already crowded (Power Glove/U-Force/LaserScope).
- DECISION: verify Intellivision vs Speedking images; leaning Intellivision for image reliability unless CCH images accessible.

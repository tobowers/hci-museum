## Phase 2: Candidate Review

### Subagent A result: Aesthedes (1984)
**Verdict:** ✨ EXCEPTIONALLY STRONG. Promote to collection.
- 636-key membrane desk (key-per-function philosophy), 6 screens (3 colour + 3 monochrome)
- 10× Motorola 68000, 220 kg, ~200-250 units, ~$150K
- Used for Dutch banknote design (25-guilder "Robin" note), Total Design, Volvo, Mercedes, Marks & Spencer
- Total Design nearly went bankrupt from buying 3 units; scrapped them in 1990
- 2 fully restored working units at HomeComputerMuseum Netherlands
- UNDO key was never implemented (but the key existed!)
- Rich sources: Wikipedia, AIS/Design academic article, Shift Happens newsletter, GoFundMe page, UvA museum
- Excellent images: Wikimedia Commons (7 photos), HomeComputerMuseum gallery, Shift Happens keyboard detail photos, YouTube videos

### Subagent B result: PARC LiveBoard (1991-92)
**Verdict:** Strong but defer. Good candidate for the future.
- 67-inch rear-projected pen display, laser beam-interruption position sensing
- Part of Weiser's Tab/Pad/Board Ubicomp triad
- 12 authors on the CHI '92 paper; Seminal Weiser SciAm '91 article
- Commercialized as LiveWorks; $49,500; ~2,000 sold by 2000
- CHM holds a physical artifact (accession #X5054.2009)
- Interaction model is genuinely absent from collection (wall-sized collaborative display)
- BUT: more corporate/large-scale than museum's usual intimate hardware artifacts. Slightly overlaps with SMART Board territory. Defer.

### Subagent D result: Accessibility devices
- **Talking Signs (1979-1991+)** — Infrared directional audio signage. Exceptionally rich documentation (SKTF Fall 1991 full issue, multiple CSUN papers, federal standard ADAAG 703.7, 40-year lineage continuing to overTHERE app in 2016). Hundreds of transmitters deployed in SF, Seattle, NYC, DC. The interaction model (directional IR beam scanning) is unique and elegant.
- **Nomad (1988)** — Audio-tactile graphics tablet. Sparse documentation. Defer.

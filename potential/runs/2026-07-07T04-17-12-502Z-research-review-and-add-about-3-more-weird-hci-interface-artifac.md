## Subagent Results (all 6 returned)

### Interactive Whiteboards (subagent 1)
- NTT ClearBoard-1 (1991): Half-silvered mirror video overlay + shared drawing. Hiroshi Ishii, NTT. ICC Tokyo has one. VERY HIGH weirdness.
- Bell Labs Electronic Blackboard (1974-82): 6×8 ft board, chalk strokes → phone tones. AT&T Gemini-100 commercial version.
- Xerox LiveBoard (1990-92): 67-inch rear-projection display, laser pen sensing, custom Colab room. CHM has one.
- SMART Board (1991): David Martin/Nancy Knowlton. First interactive whiteboard. Became mainstream.
- OKI Kawaraban (1983): Mechanical scanning whiteboard-printer. Office appliance.

### Wacom Tablets (subagent 2)
- Wacom WT-460M (1984): First cordless stylus, battery-powered.
- Wacom SD-510C (1988): First cordless+battery-free EMR tablet. G-Mark award. CHM has one. Bizarre founder story (Unification Church, spirit-world communication device). But interaction model overlaps with KoalaPad/Pencept/GRiDPad already in museum.

### Chord Keyboards (subagent 3)
- BAT Keyboard (Infogrip, 1990): 7-key chord, NASA spin-off, Israeli Air Force origins. CHM has images.
- Maltron Keyboard (1977): 3D contoured keywells, Lillian Malt story. Still hand-made. Wikimedia images.
- Kinesis Model 100 (1992): Maltron's American descendant. Hollywood appearances.
- Velotype (1982): Two-handed syllabic chord, 200 wpm. Dutch.

### Ultrasonic Controllers (subagent 4)
- Logitech 3D Mouse & Head Tracker (c.1991): Bat-sonar flying mouse, 6-DOF. Good images on vrdepot.com.
- SAC GP-8-3D / Freepoint 3D (1989): Acoustic 3D digitizer for CAD/surgery. CHM has SAC artifacts.
- De Bruyne Acoustic Radar (1980): Passive ultrasonic pulse-echo. ETH Zurich. Patent diagrams only.
- Personics Ultrasonic Head Tracker (1984-87): 40 kHz head-mounted cursor. Patent only.

### Tactile Displays (subagent 5)
- Braillotron TI-30 (1978): TI-30 calculator hacked with magnetic Braille display. Great images. HIGH weirdness.
- Optacon II (1985): Vibrotactile letter shapes. Overlaps with Optacon concept (pre-1976).
- BRAILLEX (1975): First German electronic Braille line. Deutsches Museum has prototype.
- Braille 'n Speak (1987): Speech-only notetaker, no Braille display. Cultural icon.

### Broad Weird Search (subagent 6)
- VersaWriter (1981): Pantograph drawing arm for Apple II/Atari. Mechanical analog-to-digital. Truly unique.
- Rosetronix Brainwave Control (1982): Claimed consumer BCI for Timex Sinclair 1000. Dubious.
- ComputerEyes (1984): Video frame grabber. Interesting but more bridge than interface.

### LiveBoard (subagent): IMAGES CONFIRMED
- CHM catalog #102678840: 4 professional photos by Mark Richards (800px+). © Mark Richards.
- Mark Stefik blog: 8+ historical photos (skunkworks prototype, Colab room, laser detection, meeting scenes). ~20-50KB each.
- No Wikimedia Commons images.
- CHM holds physical LB3 unit (serial 3E1122X), 54×76.5×27.5 inches.
- 12 authors on CHI '92 paper. LiveWorks spin-off, 2,000+ units sold by 2000.
- Assessment: EXCELLENT. CHM-grade photos, seminal artifact, rich documentation.

### BAT Keyboard (subagent): IMAGES CONFIRMED
- CHM catalog #102662183/#300000093: professional photos by Mark Richards.
- Hackaday Aug 2020: detailed teardown photos (Kristina Panos).
- Keyboard Wiki: 12+ gallery images including spring force comparison.
- NASA Spinoff 1993 fully verified (document ID 20020080924).
- Infogrip president: Ward Bond. Israeli Air Force origin (individual unnamed).
- Assessment: EXCELLENT. CHM-grade photos, NASA provenance verified.

### VersaWriter (subagent): IMAGES WEAK

- Ad scans from Megalextoria/AtariMania show the device but are printed ads.
- Manual PDF has diagrams.
- Company details sparse (founder unconfirmed).
- Roberta Williams used it for "Mystery House" (1980).
- Assessment: WEAK. Interesting concept but insufficient image quality for museum display.

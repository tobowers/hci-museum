## Field Note: "The Two Dials and the Two Hands" (2026-09-03)

### Decision
Wrote a Field Note on the **Sony BVE-600** (c. 1985), the desktop U-Matic editing controller with two independent jog dials — one per VCR deck, one per hand. Subject was chosen over CMX-340 (single GIZMO jog knob, less bimanual) and MCS Drum Computer (interesting but music-HCI saturated), and older unwritten candidates (Amana Touchmatic, Polaroid Digital Palette, Ascom QuickFare, Odyssey 2) which remain strong future candidates.

### Angle
The BVE-600's two jog dials are a pure bimanual interaction: left hand scrubs the source deck, right hand scrubs the recorder. The interface IS a physical map of the two-deck editing task. No mode switching, no hand-off. Cross-linked to CMX-340 (predecessor, single-knob paradigm) and implicitly to Buxton's asymmetric bimanual input research.

### Chapter
The post traces the evolution from physical tape-splice editing → CMX-340's typed command + single GIZMO jog knob → BVE-600's two-handed symmetry. The jog dial itself is noted as an influential ancestor of the scroll wheel, DJ jog wheel, and DAW scrub controls.

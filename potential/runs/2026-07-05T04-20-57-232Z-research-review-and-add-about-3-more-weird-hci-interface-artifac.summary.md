## Artifacts added to collection (2)

### 1. AtariLab (1983)
**Modality:** Sensor-based physical computing / home computer as scientific instrument
**Team:** Priscilla Laws (Dickinson College), David Egolf (15-year-old programmer), Leslie Wolf (Atari PM)
**Why:** AtariLab repurposed the joystick port's paddle-reading POKEY chip as a general-purpose ADC for temperature and light probes. It was physical computing 30 years before Arduino. Killed by the Atari collapse in 1984, but the concept lived on through Vernier Software's ULI (40,000 units sold) — making it the direct ancestor of the modern probeware industry. Rich team story: physics professor + teenage coder + product manager who told her team to "keep working until you don't get a paycheck."
**Images:** 3 local images in assets/wiki/ (CC BY-SA 2.5 from Wikimedia + Antic magazine)
**Files:** potential/atarilab/info.json, assets/wiki/atarilab-*.jpg/jpeg

### 2. SGI Dial and Button Box (1986)
**Modality:** Dedicated physical controls / two-handed bimanual 3D interaction
**Team:** Silicon Graphics, Inc. (internal hardware tools group). OEM by Danaher Controls
**Why:** Eight optical encoder dials and 32 buttons representing a lost design philosophy — that professional creative tools deserve dedicated, task-specific physical controls. Used by animators at Alias, Wavefront, and Softimage for 3D navigation with zero-latency muscle memory. Bill Buxton's group used it for foundational bimanual HCI research at U of Toronto. The mapping convention (3 rotate + zoom + 2 pan + 2 aux) survives in every modern 3D application, but the knobs are gone.
**Images:** 3 local images in assets/wiki/ (CC BY-SA 3.0 from Wikimedia)
**Files:** potential/sgi-dial-box/info.json, assets/wiki/sgi-dial-box-*.jpg

# Field Notes Writing Run — 2026-07-08

## Decision

Wrote one Field Note: **"The Box That Said Yes, Boss"** about the Butler in a Box (1983).

## Rationale

Reviewed all 14 existing Field Notes against recent collection additions (past 4 curation runs, 13 new exhibits added). The Butler in a Box was the strongest candidate:

- No existing Field Note covers voice-controlled home automation or wake-word interaction
- Rich, specific origin story: professional magician Gus Searcy challenged at a Super Bowl party → partners with ex-IBM programmer Franz Kavan
- Pioneered the wake-word-plus-command model 30 years before Alexa ("Godfrey" / "Hobson" → "Yes, boss" → command)
- Extraordinary detail density: 4 voice profiles, 256 functions, 42+42 devices, if-then logic, CVSD military-grade speech chips, epoxy-sealed security module, 134-page manual, $1,495 price, Epcot Future House, William Shatner, Smithsonian artifact
- Perfect museum tone match: commercially failed but historically prescient, specific over smooth, honest about failure
- Emotional hook: Searcy still alive in 2024, offering PIN recovery on a YouTube comment for a device he built 41 years earlier

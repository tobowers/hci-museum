# Agent Scout Trace

Topic: research, review, and add about 3 more weird HCI interface artifacts from 1976-1992

## Run: 2026-08-22

### Phase 1: Subagent Research (3 parallel subagents)

**Subagent 1** (musical instrument HCI):
- Starr Labs Ztar (1986) — key-based fretboard guitar controller, no strings. Image-blocked (no CC images on Commons). Strong candidate but image-blocked.
- Crumar GDS (1980) — Z80/CP/M synth lab with reconfigurable slider/pot control surface. 5-10 units. Image-blocked.
- Synclavier I keyboardless config (1977) — terminal-only interaction. Has CC images but overlaps with existing music HCI entries.

**Subagent 2** (dedicated info appliances):
- Seiko UC-2000 (1984) — wrist-worn info system with dock-based keyboard. Shares overlap with Seiko RC-1000 already in museum.
- Epson HX-20 (1982) — first notebook computer. Good CC images. Interaction is notebook with integrated printer + microcassette.
- HP-75C/D (1982) — magnetic card reader calculator. No Commons images found.
- Amstrad NC100 (1992) — ROM-resident instant-on word processor. Has CC images. Edge of window.

**Subagent 3** (weird game controllers):
- Atari 2700 (1981) — wireless RF joystick/paddle hybrid, cancelled prototype. No Commons images.
- Atari 5200 controller (1982) — non-centering analog joystick. STRONG. Multiple CC images by Evan-Amos.
- Atari Mindlink (1984) — already in museum.

### Phase 2: Candidate Selection

Selected primary candidate: **Atari 5200 Controller (1982)**
- Non-centering analog joystick with rubber boot mechanism (no spring return)
- 12-button keypad with game-specific overlays
- Two fire buttons, start/pause/reset buttons
- 1 million units sold (commercially released)
- IGN's 10th worst controller
- Multiple CC images from Evan-Amos on Wikimedia Commons
- Strong HCI story: the rubber-boot centering mechanism was a usability disaster

Attempted to find 2 additional strong candidates:
- Epson HX-20 (1982) — good images but interaction is standard keyboard+LCD, not "weird"
- Amstrad NC100 (1992) — edge of window, not sufficiently weird
- Atari 2700 (1981) — no CC images
- HP-75C (1982) — no CC images
- Seiko UC-2000 (1984) — overlaps with Seiko RC-1000

### Decision: Promote Atari 5200 Controller as a single strong addition
Adding 1 strong candidate is better than padding with 2-3 weak ones. The Atari 5200 controller has a genuinely unique HCI story, good images, and clean differentiation from all existing exhibits.

# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Creative technologists, interaction designers, HCI researchers, and nostalgic tech enthusiasts — people who are drawn to the history of interfaces and love discovering that someone built a brain-controlled game controller in 1984. They arrive curious and want to be surprised.

## Product Purpose

A multi-page digital museum celebrating the golden age of HCI experimentation (late 1970s – early 1990s). Each exhibit combines factual research with a deep, visually rich dive into a specific project. The site itself is an artifact: it should feel like walking through a beautifully preserved science-center exhibition where every panel has a CRT glow.

## Brand Personality

Exhibition retro: authoritative but playful, like a science-museum display from an alternate 1986. Precise typography, phosphor-green interface accents, archival photography, and scanline warmth. The voice is knowledgeable, slightly awestruck, and never ironic.

Three words: archival, luminous, tactile.

## Anti-references

- Generic cyberpunk neon (purple/blue gradients, chrome, rain).
- SaaS marketing polish (rounded cards, soft shadows, gradient blobs).
- Hacker-terminal parody (matrix rain, unreadable green-on-black novelty).
- Plain Wikipedia-style information pages without visual texture.

## Design Principles

1. **The museum is the interface.** Every page should feel like an exhibition space, not a website wrapped around content.
2. **Show the artifact first.** Lead with original hardware, photographs, or period graphics before explaining context.
3. **Light is state.** Glow, scanlines, and motion respond to interaction; static decoration is noise.
4. **Period accuracy with contemporary craft.** Use the visual language of the era (monospace, CRT green, technical labels) but execute it with modern precision.
5. **Surprise without gimmicks.** Weird projects deserve a strange, beautiful presentation, but never at the cost of readability.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- Target WCAG 2.1 AA.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- Ensure phosphor-green text has sufficient contrast against dark backgrounds.
- Provide alt text for all archival imagery.
